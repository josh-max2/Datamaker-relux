    Frequently asked questions: AFC vs KFC

    Is AFC cheaper than KFC to start?

    Based on the initial franchise fee disclosed in each franchisor's most recent FDD, KFC has the lower starting fee — about $0 less than AFC. This compares only the franchise fee itself, not the total investment range (which includes build-out, equipment, working capital, and other line items). See the comparison table above for both totals.

    Which has the lower total investment, AFC or KFC?

    The franchisor-disclosed total investment range is $955,500–$1,519,500 for AFC and $2,107,575–$4,155,000 for KFC. By midpoint, AFC has the lower investment requirement. Your actual cost depends on location, build-out specifics, and whether you self-finance — see each brand's full Item 7 disclosure for the line-item breakdown.

    What royalty does AFC vs KFC charge?

    AFC charges 6% and KFC charges 4% of gross sales. KFC has the lower royalty rate. Royalty differences compound: 1 percentage point on $500k gross sales = $5,000 per year.

    Which has more locations, AFC or KFC?

    As of the latest reporting year, AFC reported 386 outlets and KFC reported 3,490. KFC is the larger system. Bigger isn't automatically better — it can mean more brand recognition but also a more saturated territory map. Check each brand's outlet-growth chart for the trend over time.

    Do both AFC and KFC disclose Item 19 earnings?

    Yes — both franchisors include Item 19 Financial Performance Representations in their most recent FDDs. AFC reports 25 cohort × metric records; KFC reports 1. See each brand's Item 19 table for the full breakdown.
